## Disturbing New Direction
This title is one of the most unusual and psychologically disturbing entries in the legendary survival horror franchise. Even though the franchise already had very intense games on this regard, I can safely say that this is one of the most disturbing games I've ever experienced. Taking a different direction than the previous entries, it abandons many of the familiar elements that defined the series and introduces its unique approach. Rather than immediately placing you in a fog-covered town, the game begins in am ordinary apartment, creating an atmosphere of isolation and claustrophobia. And this is amplified by the first person perspective, never used before in the franchise. It likes to experiment with the established formula, and it ended up making it one of the most distinctive survival horror experiences of its generation.

## Henry's Nightmare
The MC is called Henry Townshend, an ordinary man who finds himself trapped inside his apartment, Room 302. His windows cannot be opened, his front door is chained shut from the inside, and all communication has been cut off. A mysterious hole appears in his bathroom wall, offering a passage into strange an alternate dimensions, and this is where the perspective shifts into the traditional 3rd person. As you explores these unfamiliar environments, he gradually uncovers a disturbing series of events connected to the main villain, called Walter Sullivan. The narrative is unsettling, filled with disturbing imagery, supernatural occurrences, and psychological themes. This is something that fans of the franchise should already be familiar with, but trust me when I say this, there is nothing like this particular game, even when it comes to the Silent Hill franchise as a whole.

## Apartment Turned Hell
One o its highlights is definitely the atmosphere, it takes a different approach by making the apartment an important part of the experience, and not just a traditional "safe room". Initially presented as a safe place, but it gradually becomes more and more disturbing, with some heavy imagery and supernatural events. Strange noises, unexplained apparitions, mysterious figures, and other events slowly transform the apartment into another source of terror. Particularly what makes this game so horrifying for me is the sound design, which alone can carry the whole tension behind every step. This loss of security is effective because it challenges the your expectations and creates an uncomfortable feeling that there is nowhere left to hide. The first-person perspective also establishes a more intimate connection, making even seemingly insignificant details feel suspicious.

## Exploring Otherworldly Realms
The alternate worlds are another major highlight, offering a variety of memorable locations that contribute to the game's identity. From abandoned subway stations and eerie forests to haunted apartment buildings and underground prisons, each location has its own personality. Although they are generally more linear than previous entries, they still manage to deliver plenty of memorable moments through their environmental storytelling. The creature designs are equally impressive, featuring some of the franchise's strangest and most uncomfortable enemies. Certain supernatural entities are particularly memorable because of their unusual movements and resistance to conventional combat. Though I really think that the immortal Ghost enemy is simply not a good fit for the franchise, this kind of enemy can ruin the fun out o the exploration sections.

## Sound and Atmosphere
The sound design and musical composition deserve special recognition as well. It mostly has industrial noises, distorted sounds, and haunting melodies that perfectly complement the game's disturbing environments. I can't stress this enough, but the sound design alone, is what carries the most terrifying aspects of this title, for me personally. The audio is particularly effective during moments when you are exploring empty locations, creating a constant sense of tension without relying on jump scares. The voice acting is generally solid, but it is particularly impressive for a SH title since most of them have really bad quality VA. Even after more than two decades, the game's audio and visual direction remain incredibly effective at creating an uncomfortable atmosphere.

## Walter Sullivan's Story
The narrative is one of the most interesting aspects of the entire experience. The main villain is a very disturbing antagonist, and his connection to the events surrounding Room 302 gives the story a much darker and more personal dimension. His tragic background and disturbing motivations create a villain who is quite different from the more traditional supernatural threats of the genre as a whole. The game also explores themes of loneliness, obsession, childhood trauma, and the corruption of innocence, which is already a traditional trait of every Silent Hill entry, mainline or not. While some narrative elements can feel confusing and obscure, the mystery surrounding Walter and the strange events around Henry makes the experience very engaging. And while you get a lot of information from notes and documents, I recommend watching some videos explaining the symbolism behind the story, which for me, this habit is already a part of playing this kind of game.

## Flawed Gameplay Mechanics
Unfortunately, the gameplay is where this title struggles, although the basic combat and exploration mechanics are familiar to fans of the genre, the controls can feel clunky, particularly when dealing with multiple enemies. But at the same time, the game also have some mechanics that you can exploit, such as invulnerability frames when performing a charged melee attack. The limited inventory can become frustrating, forcing you to carefully manage their available items and occasionally do some backtracking. And although this is a common trait for the genre, this title can be particularly unpredictable. Some enemies are unnecessarily difficult to deal with, while others can become more irritating than frightening after repeated encounters. This is not something specific to this 4th entry, but this one in particular, I think is the worst throught the whole franchise.

## Revisiting the Nightmare
Another controversial aspect is the second half of the game, which requires you to revisit previously explored locations. Although this decision is connected to the narrative and introduces additional challenges. And while some consider this to make the game repetitive, I think it adds enough of a twist to make revisiting most areas an interesting experience. But having to travel through familiar environments again, while dealing with escort mechanics, can become exhausting. The introduction of Eileen as a companion adds another layer to the gameplay, but protecting her throughout certain sections can be frustrating. What helped a lot is to try to find the unique weapons that she can use, which can make her quite an effective fighter. These mechanics are not necessarily terrible ideas, but their execution could have been considerably better.

## Unforgettable Experience
Despite these shortcomings, the 4th mainline entry remains an example of what made the PlayStation 2 era such a great period for survival horror. Rather than simply repeating the formula established by the previous games, it attempted something different, creating an experience that feels uncomfortable and even surreal. Its unique and unconventional structure and disturbing atmosphere gives it a unique identity that separates it from every other entry in the franchise. Some may argue that this shouldn't even be a Silent Hill title, but something else entirely, so that it could spawn a new franchise of its own. It might not be as polished as the 2nd entry, nor does it have the same level of exploration as its predecessors, but it compensates with an atmosphere and narrative that are difficult to forget.
