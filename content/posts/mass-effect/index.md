## Engaging Science Fiction
The first Mass Effect entry is a landmark science fiction RPGs that delivers an engaging story, memorable characters, and a richly developed universe. The MC is called Commander Shepard, an elite human soldier tasked with stopping a rogue Spectre named Saren, whose actions threaten the stability of the galaxy. And the premise alone is already highly engaging, with a totally original universe filled with different races, aliens, humans, with different backgrounds and cultures. What begins as a straightforward investigation gradually progresses into an adventure involving ancient civilizations, political tensions, and a danger far greater than anyone initially realizes. The narrative is one of its highlights, combining the scale of a space opera with conflicts and decisions that gives you a sense of responsibility, although this is common for today's standards, it was very impactful at the time.

## Rich World-Building
One of the most impressive aspects is its world-building, the game introduces a fascinating setting populated by diverse alien species, each with its own history, culture, political interests, and relationships with humanity. Exploring the Citadel, visiting different planets, and speaking with the various characters makes the universe feel alive and believable. Although most of the time, you can't really explore absolutely all the planets, you can at least read about them while visiting their locations. The squad members are memorable, with distinct personalities and backgrounds that make them feel like more than just companions in combat. This is also reflected in the dialog options you get, depending on which squad you decide to go with on each mission. I'm a big fan of Wrex and Garrus, they are as black and white as you can get, Wrex being pure renegade and Garrus being pure paragon.

## Choices and Consequences
The game's decision-making system is another major highlight, conversations frequently offer several responses, allowing you to portray Shepard as diplomatic, pragmatic, aggressive, or somewhere in between. This system has become a trademark of the Bioware team, and was used in a lot of subsequent titles. Important choices can have significant consequences, and the game really makes these decisions feel complicated rather than presenting obvious right and wrong answers. But if want to automate things you can simply always choose the most impartial route and you'll still be fine. And although there is the ability to import your character and decisions into the subsequent games, I admit that I never engaged with this feature, but I do recognize this is a very unique and interesting trait.

## Outdated Gameplay Mechanics
Unfortunately, the gameplay has not aged quite as much as the world-building. Combat combines shooting with RPG mechanics, squad commands, and biotic or technological abilities, offering several ways to approach encounters, giving it a sense of being a very tactical game. While the underlying systems can be enjoyable, the gunplay sometimes feels clumsy, the cover mechanics are awkward, and the artificial intelligence can be unreliable. The biggest issue is the aiming mechanic, which you don't really have full control of where you are shooting. Inventory management is another downside, as the large amount of equipment and frequent comparisons between similar items can interrupt the pace. Another problem is the limited inventory space, that often forces you to dispose or sell items. Exploration is also very limited, particularly when driving the Mako, and although the vehicle sections adds to the feeling of exploring, many of the optional locations are very repetitive.

## Atmospheric Presentation
The presentation is similarly a mixture of strengths and weaknesses, for a 2007 game, Mass Effect features impressive cinematic dialogue scenes and a distinctive visual identity. Its OST deserves some praise, blending atmospheric electronic music with orchestral elements which fits perfectly with the space opera theme. The environments and character animations can look dated by modern standards, and some locations can feel very blend, but the art direction is effective enough to sustain the atmosphere. The original PC version also has its share of technical quirks, although its mouse-and-keyboard controls and interface offer advantages over the original console release.

## Timeless Science Fiction
Ultimately, the first Mass Effect entry remains an outstanding RPG whose greatest achievements lie in its storytelling, atmosphere, and sense of adventure. Its combat, inventory systems, and side content can be frustrating, and some design aspects are very outdated, but these shortcomings rarely overshadow the quality of its standout elements. Bioware successfully created a science fiction universe that feels worth exploring, populated it with companions worth knowing, and gave you the opportunity to influence its future. This is a must play if you are a science fiction fan, but I highly recommend the Legendary Edition instead of the original one. I also deserves a lot of praise in the narrative-driven RPGs mechanics, which even today, earned a lasting influence on the genre.
