## An Improved Experience
This title is an action-adventure game released as an expanded and improved version of the original Castlevania 64. Set in the familiar gothic world of Dracula, the game delivers a dark and atmospheric experience filled with haunted forests, mysterious mansions and all sorts of supernatural creatures. And while its presentation is very limited for today's standards, it definitely made an impression back in the day, especially due to its horror elements. It builds upon the foundations of its predecessor by introducing new content, improved visuals, additional gameplay sections, and new playable characters. It really feels like they learned from their mistakes in the first title, and tried way harder this time around, even though it is still not an amazing Castlevania entry.

## Atmosphere and Presentation
One of the game's greatest strengths is its atmosphere, the environments do an excellent job of capturing the dark fantasy aesthetic that is always one of the key aspects of the Castlevania franchise. From fog-covered outdoor areas to creepy interiors and enormous castle corridors, the game creates a sense of isolation. Although like most games at the time, the fog visuals were more related to technical limitations, this time around it serves like a good trait for the tone of this game. The OST also deserves praise, complementing the environments with haunting compositions, similar to every, but not all, Castlevania entries. Despite the console's technical limitations, the developers managed to create a world that feels mysterious and unsettling.

## Cornell Takes the Spotlight
The biggest addition is the MC Cornell, a werewolf warrior who serves as the main protagonist of the new storyline. His campaign provides a different perspective on the events surrounding Dracula's return and introduces a more action-oriented approach to combat. This character practically carries this title on its own, his gameplay is far superior than the characters from the first N64 entry, while its story is way more interesting too. The game also includes expanded campaigns featuring Reinhardt Schneider and Carrie Fernandez, the playable characters from the previous entry, offering additional content. Though the only optional content I would recommend is the Henry one, because it really changes how you play the game. The other two characters simply play the Cornell's campaign in his place.

## Gameplay and Technical Issues
Gameplay is a mixture of exploration, platforming, puzzle-solving, and combat. You navigate interconnected environments, discover keys, overcome obstacles, and battle a variety of monsters along the way. It is not the best possible transition from the 2D to 3D, but it was the best they could manage at the time. There is a satisfying sense of progression as new areas become accessible, despite it not having any complex enough mechanic to make it more interesting. However, the controls haven't aged particularly well, movement can feel stiff, combat is sometimes awkward. And the camera is the worst part, similar to other 3D titles of the time, though you can lock-on to enemies, which is a feature that was ahead of its time. The platforming sections can be especially frustrating when precise jumps are required.

## Old-School Charm
Despite these shortcomings, there is something genuinely appealing about the game's old-school design. Its slower pace, challenging encounters, and emphasis on exploration give it a distinctive personality that separates it from many modern action-adventure games. Although its cutscenes have no voice-acting, and the boss encounters are not particularly difficult, it still manages to deliver a very satisfying experience. It also retains much of the charm associated with late-1990s gaming, when developers were still experimenting with translating established 2D franchises into 3D experiences. If anything, this is a complete upgrade when compared to the first N64 entry, even though it is not a good comparison since it was a somewhat disaster of a game.

## Imperfect but Enjoyable
Ultimately, the 2nd N64 Castlevania entry is an imperfect but enjoyable entry in the franchise and arguably the more complete version of the Nintendo 64 Castlevania experience. Its technical flaws and occasionally frustrating mechanics are difficult to overlook, but its impressive atmosphere, memorable OST and very good dark fantasy presentation makes it worth experiencing. For fans of classic Castlevania games and Nintendo 64 enthusiasts, this is definitely a must-play. Especially those willing to look past its dated controls and camera issues. And if you are well versed in retro gaming, these will be minor issues that will absolutely not hurt the overall experience this is title trying to deliver.
